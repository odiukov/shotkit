# Failure modes — the full debug catalog (reference)

The complete symptom → cause → fix table for renders made from a shotkit project. Consult
this file when a generated image/clip came out wrong; the top authoring-time reminders live
in SKILL.md ("Failure modes"). Fix = rewrite the offending field (`scenePrompt` or
`motionPrompt`) and re-run — do not switch engines; quality lives in the prompt structure and
the reference images.

## Dynamism — the clip is boring

The most common defect, and the most expensive: a technically clean render in which nothing
happens. Prevent it at authoring time with the dynamism gate (SKILL.md → "The dynamism gate").

| Symptom | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| The clip is beautiful but **boring** — actors stand and breathe, camera watches, nothing occurs | No motion channel in the shots and no WORLD event in the clip. Usually the beat was written as an emotional *state* ("she realizes…"), which has nothing to render | Give every shot a channel — BODY (travels/applies force), CAMERA (frame moves), or WORLD (something changes) — and every clip at least one WORLD event. See "The three motion channels" |
| A quiet/emotional beat renders as a person standing still | The emotion was written with nothing to play it against | Give the character an **activity**: she realizes she's been betrayed *while packing his suitcase*. Put the subtext in HOW the activity is done (folding carefully → jamming it in), not in the face. See "Play the emotion against an ACTIVITY" |
| 15 seconds, one short line, and a lot of nothing | Dead air — `durationSec` was set first and the beat did not fill it | The word budget is `durationSec × ~2`. If the beat is shorter than the clock, **cut `durationSec`** — never pad with holding. Enter shots late, leave early; aim for a nameable happening every ~3s |
| The clip cuts, but the cuts feel pointless — nothing changed | Cut between two settled poses (two photographs in sequence), and/or adjacent shots at the same size and height | Cut **ON** the action so the movement straddles the edit ("cut ON the reach"), and vary size AND height/angle across the cut. See "Cut ON the action" |
| The camera never moves in the whole clip | The locked-off frame was the reflex, not a choice — the old "locked-off beats a drone" prior | Default to a **motivated** move: follow the activity, push on the turn, rack to what changed. A locked frame is correct only when the stillness is the point (HELD register), and you should be able to say why |
| Everything is HELD — every clip is one long take | HELD was defaulted into rather than chosen | HELD is a *purchase*: you spend the whole clip on stillness, so the stillness must BE the event. Otherwise use CHARGED (3–4 cuts, the drama default) or KINETIC (5–6). See "Energy registers" |
| Somebody spends the first half of the clip **walking toward** the place the scene happens | The scene started too early: the approach was filmed as preamble, so the opening seconds had to be paid with travel — and a mostly-locomotion prompt makes the engine add pace of its own | Open on the **arrival**: she stands at the door, not walking to it. The viewer infers the journey from the state. Travel earns a shot only when the path IS the event (chase, escape, a walk that costs her something). See "Start on the ARRIVAL, not the approach" |
| **The FIRST shot of the clip drags** — it feels twice as long as its timecode, and the clip reads slow before anything happens | A slow, low-event beat (a drift-in, a settling pose, a wordless approach) was put in shot 1: on t2v the opening shot gets a disproportionate share of frames and the model settles into it | Open shot 1 on an action **already in motion**, or on a fast reveal/entrance. Move any slow drift out of the opening slot. See the dead-air rule |
| Two adjacent shots are both "she approaches" at different sizes | The approach was cut into coverage instead of being cut out | One shot at most, and it must turn (arrive, stop, be interrupted). Better: delete it and start on the arrival |
| **Characters are running / pacing / storming out** in scenes that don't call for it | Over-correcting the dead-scene rule with locomotion — the BODY channel got read as "make them walk somewhere", and "no shot may merely continue" got read as "escalate the speed" | A dynamic is a **change**, not a **velocity**. Take the lowest rung of the fix ladder: the *activity changes* (same posture, different how), then a small WORLD event, then a camera turn. Crosses and Acts are rungs 4–5 — hook and reversals only. See "The fix ladder" |
| Every scene has a slap/shove/smash — it plays as soap opera | Over-correcting the boredom fix with the Act tier everywhere | Acts belong on the hook and the reversals ONLY. The default tier is an **activity** plus one event. See the three tiers |
| A strong action (slam, shove, throw) renders **rubbery / weightless** | Amplitude with no physics — the force has no contact point and no consequence | Pay the **force triad**: force applied → point of contact → consequence in the world ("shoulder into the door — the hinges bang the frame, dust jumping off the lintel"). The fix is more physics, never less action |
| A shot just shows the previous shot still going — she is still walking, he is still watching | The shot was written as duration, not as a beat: it continues an action instead of turning it | Every shot **starts, changes, interrupts or lands** the action. If shot N's only content is "shot N-1, later", cut it or give it the turn. See "No shot may merely CONTINUE" |
| A whole scene plays as a mood interlude — nobody does anything | A dead scene: written as atmosphere or as a held moment, with no happening on camera | Only an **idle loop scene** may be a dead hold. Give every scene a beat — an activity, an event, a decision landing on camera. See "no dead scene" |
| The prompt contains "in silence" / "neither speaking" / "they stand and look at each other" / "the tension builds" | The phrasing IS the defect — none of these name a happening, so the engine renders posed actors | Replace each phrase with the action it was gesturing at. Ban-list and rewrites in SKILL.md → "The tells that you have written a dead scene" |
| A static single that can't be fixed (vertical frame, nowhere to move) | Nothing between camera and subject | Add **foreground parallax** — a body crossing close to the lens, a swinging door, traffic, steam. Reads as motion and depth for almost no words |

## Body, performance, camera

| Symptom | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Actor floats / no weight | No body-weight cue in the prompt | Add "weight on her right hip" / "shoulders dropped" / "hand braced" |
| Hair/fabric/environment static | No environmental force | Add a wind/gravity/surface line to the prompt |
| Face over-acted, telegraphed | Verbs "cries", "smiles widely", "rages" | Replace with micro-actions: "swallows once", "corner of mouth lifts then settles" |
| Camera flies/drones/swoops | "sweeping"/"epic"/"drone"/"cinematic movement" in `motionPrompt` | Replace with a **motivated, grounded move** — "handheld follow at her shoulder", "dolly-in at walking pace", "push in as the line lands". Don't over-correct to a locked frame: the fix for an unmotivated move is a motivated one, not stillness |
| Averted-gaze speaking reads vacant / dead-eyed | "Looks away from the listener" gives the eyes nothing to fix on | Anchor the averted gaze to a real object — "eyes drop to the glass in her hand", "gaze on the rain down the window" — eyes on SOMETHING, not nowhere |
| Driving scene = music video | Camera moves too much | Lock the frame; gentle handheld breathing only |
| Scene reads as soundstage | No environmental force AND no ambient | Add force to the prompt + `generateAudio:true` |

## Cutting, pacing, optics

| Symptom | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Clip reads flat / no cinematography / no shot changes | `motionPrompt` is a single continuous take | Rewrite as an inline hard-cut sequence in the right register — CHARGED (3–4 cuts) for drama, KINETIC (5–6) for a hook/reversal/action beat — with varied sizes AND heights (see "Multi-shot by default"); keep `@mentions` in every shot. Check the beat has content to cut *to*: if every shot is the same person standing, the cuts won't save it — fix the dynamism gate first |
| Multi-shot clip smears / cuts blur into one move / shots indistinct | Too many cuts for the duration — >~6 cuts in a 15s clip, individual shots under ~1.5–2s have too few frames to read | Cut the cut count to ~1 per 2–3s (≤~6 in 15s); if the beat needs more coverage, split it into a second scene. See "Pace to duration" |
| A beat you wanted as ONE held take (oner / intimacy / driving lock / loop) cuts to other angles on its own | Most engines DEFAULT to cutting — they insert angle changes unless told not to | Write "ONE continuous shot, NO cuts, no zoom" explicitly in the `motionPrompt`. See the held-single-take exception |
| Edit feels restless / anxious / cuts too often (drama) | Cutting for variety, not motivation — chasing "change the plan every cut" as a quota | Cut on motivation only, and drop to the CHARGED register (3–4 cuts in 10–15s). But check the opposite first: a long shot only "breathes" if something is *happening* in it — holding on a person who is doing nothing is dead air, not restraint |
| Shots feel samey / no sense of power or depth despite varied SIZE | Camera held at one height/angle across the clip (all eye-level) | Vary the second axis — camera height/angle (low/eye/high/overhead) by who holds status this beat — and pin the lens by FOV in degrees. See "Vary the ANGLE/HEIGHT too" |
| Fog/rain/dust reads thin or absent despite the prompt asking for it | Atmosphere given as a mood word ("light fog"), not a measured density | Quantify it — "fog density 40%", "haze at 15 m depth" — and ramp the number across shots for a build (20%→40%→60%). See "Quantify what the engine measures" |
| A "fast" car / "slow" pan renders floaty or wrong-paced | Speed left as an adjective | State km/h for subject and camera separately ("car at 90 km/h", "camera pans at 5 km/h") |
| **A character RUNS when the prompt said walk** ("strides", "climbs fast", "brisk walking pace") | Gait given as an adjective — the engine takes the fastest plausible reading; a tight follow ("breathing with her stride") amplifies it further | Quantify the gait and lock it: "walks at an even 4 km/h, unhurried walking pace — walking throughout, never running, never jogging", repeated in LOCKS. See "A HUMAN's gait is a number too" |
| A character runs even though the pace WAS quantified | The route is longer than `durationSec` allows at that pace, so the engine speeds the body up to cover it | Fit the distance to the clock, not the clock to the distance: start them already most of the way along the route, or cut to the arrival. See "Start on the ARRIVAL, not the approach" |
| Colour temperature drifts warm/cool across hard cuts | WB named as a mood ("warm light"), not locked | Lock the WB in Kelvin and repeat the number every shot (`WB locked 5600K`). See Multi-shot continuity §1 |
| Extreme-FOV clip (8° tele / 107° ultra-wide) loses its optics after 2–3 cuts and drifts back to a neutral look | On t2v there's no per-cut keyframe to hold the lens; a single FOV mention at the top decays | Restate the FOV at the START and END of every shot line — LENS LOCK opener + LENS CHECK closer. See "Cross-cut locks" |
| A whip-pan renders as a plain hard cut with no motion blur | The whip was given under ~0.8s — too few frames to smear | Give the whip ≥0.8s across three beats (A settled → whip → B settled); for a true instant edit write a HARD CUT instead. See "Optical recipes … & transition timing" |

## Identity & who-is-who

| Symptom | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Face/place/prop drifts across shots | Relying on prose, no reference image | Attach a reference and `@mention` the id in every shot |
| Multi-character frame: identities/positions scramble | Loose refs animated; a character has an empty `canonicalDescription` so shotkit's auto who-is-who clause can't name it | Stay on the default path: ensure every `@mentioned` character has a short clean `canonicalDescription`, and cut fewer shots. Don't hand-duplicate the trait inline. Only for a hard lock: opt into a seeded mode (compose + approve a keyframe first) |
| Two characters SWAPPED on the default path (Caroline rendered as Lana) even though they look different | One of them has an empty/missing `canonicalDescription`, so shotkit's auto who-is-who clause can't bind that face to its name | Give every `@mentioned` character a short clean `canonicalDescription` — shotkit injects it as the who-is-who clause. For a hard lock switch that shot to a seeded mode. See "Who-is-who on the default path" |
| Background/extra characters all wear the protagonist's face (a crowd of clones) | t2v has ONE human reference image attached; with no other face refs the model stamps that face onto every person in frame | Describe extras as a BACKGROUND CROWD of DISTINCT strangers — varied faces/hair/build, "none of them the protagonist" — render them heavily OUT OF FOCUS, faces turned away / in profile, only the lead in sharp focus. Add "do NOT copy @lead's face onto the background" (the blur/averting does the work; the negative alone is weak) |
| A face only seen mid-clip (a hard cut to a tight single of a different character) is invented or drifts | On t2v a cut to a new tight face has no keyframe to seed it, so the model guesses that identity | Give each new face its own seed: either put that beat in its OWN scene, or stay inside one clip starting from a two-shot / over-the-shoulder that already contains both faces — don't hard-cut to a fresh tight single of someone the clip hasn't established |
| An inserted close-up of a HAND/limb doing the acting renders as the WRONG person's hand | A faceless limb insert has no identity anchor on t2v; with another character's ref attached the model may assign that limb to them | Keep the limb IN a shot that also shows its owner's face ("her face and her wiping hand both in the same frame") and bind it in words ("with the back of HER OWN hand"). Reserve faceless inserts for objects |
| Model invents jewellery / a ring / watch / accessory that should not be there (and it varies shot to shot) | The model adds plausible accessories to bare hands/wrists/necks; each cut re-rolls what it invents | Ban them globally in `style.banned` ("rings, jewelry, bracelets, necklaces, earrings") AND state "bare hands, no rings, no jewellery" in any shot with visible hands. Check the anchored refs too — if the ref image wears the accessory, it propagates; re-anchor a clean frame |

## State, props, continuity

| Symptom | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Prop changes (handbag→tote) | Prop has no reference | Add a prop entry with a `uri` pointing at a saved reference image, then `@mention` it |
| Cross-cut state/blocking drifts (bag strap swaps shoulder, chair tucks/untucks, skin tone re-tints per angle) inside ONE multi-shot clip | No per-cut keyframe on the default path to pin per-clip state; refs hold identity but not state/position | Add a positive **locks block** to the `motionPrompt` tail — 2–4 invariants as "X stays …" assertions. See "Cross-cut locks" |
| A recurring stain / dirt / wet patch / wound / torn wardrobe drifts shot-to-shot and scene-to-scene | It's a continuity entity with NO reference anchor — prose re-invents it every frame; the character ref shows the clean garment | Treat it like a prop: pull a clean frame of the stain (from a rendered clip, or generated fresh), save it under `refs/`, add a prop entry pointing at it, and `@mention` it from the scene it appears onward. Mind the ref budget |
| A stain/wound/wardrobe-state is present in shot 1 but GONE after a hard cut, even with the prop `@mentioned` | (a) The action *implied removal* — a "wipes / cleans / brushes away" gesture renders it gone; (b) a state anchored from a CLOSE-UP under-applies on a wider shot, and every cut re-rolls it | Don't write a removal-implying verb for a state you need to KEEP ("flicks a crumb but does NOT wipe the stain away"). Re-assert the exact state in EVERY shot's text plus a global "the stain is identical in every shot and never disappears". Prefer fewer cuts for a fragile state |
| A character is rendered in the WRONG clothes — not the outfit their ref/look shows (ref = black hoodie, render = grey tee) | A concrete garment word in the prompt ("in a fitted team tee", "the shove landing on his shirt") — in refs-only (t2v) the model reads it as an INSTRUCTION and it OVERRIDES the ref, re-clothing them. Bites a single character too, not just multi-character | Name NO garment for a ref-fixed character: describe only the action/state and let the ref dictate the costume ("the shove landing square on his chest"). Positive-lock it if needed ("each in exactly their reference-sheet wardrobe — no restyling, no changed clothing"). A real wardrobe change = a look tag (`@id#label`) or a prop ref, never a bare adjective. Sweep the whole episode for leftover garment nouns |
| Physical interaction reads as discontinuous (hand passes over an object, next cut it's already knocked over/spilled) | The cause and its effect were split across a HARD CUT — two unrelated states instead of one continuous force | Keep the contact AND its result in ONE continuous shot: "single continuous take, NO cut inside this shot", keep the acting hand "in contact through" the motion, describe cause→effect as one connected movement. Cut only AFTER the action completes |
| Hands melt/extra-finger during fine-motor work (tying, unlocking, threading, writing) | Two hands manipulating something small, held on-camera, is the hardest render | Prefer ONE hand. Don't film the manipulation itself — cut to the reaction/result. If both hands are unavoidable, simplify/slow the pose and keep them out of tight focus |
| The ACT of writing/typing glitches (pen-hand warps, page/letters crawl) | The model can't render live handwriting or text forming | Don't show the writing happening: page/screen out of focus or below frame, show only the hand arriving/leaving, cut to the face. (On-screen finished text is a post overlay) |
| Subject walks the wrong way / geography reversed (enters → looks like leaving) | Ambiguous travel vector + camera position | State it explicitly: camera position ("camera behind her"), travel vector ("walks AWAY from camera, deeper into frame"), destination position ("the building ahead of her"). Pin static set pieces ("the gates are open and static") |
| Characters swap SIDES of frame / teleport across cuts or scenes | No screen-direction discipline — camera crossed the 180° line, or each scene was authored in isolation | Pick one line of action per location, state each character's frame-side + eyeline in EVERY shot/scene, keep a blocking ledger and carry the exit state forward. See "Spatial continuity & screen direction" |
| A character who moved (to the window/door) is back at the old spot next shot/scene | Movement wasn't tracked; the next prompt silently reset their position | Treat movement as one-way: update the ledger and write the new position into every subsequent shot/scene until they move again |
| Editing one scene leaves it contradicting the next (positions/props/wardrobe don't line up across the cut) | Single-prompt edit not propagated to dependents | Re-scan the neighbours + same-location scenes and rewrite the dependent chain so entry/exit states match. See "Edit propagation" |

## t2v ref binding & prompt hygiene

| Symptom | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Location drifts on the default path even though `shotkit lint` shows it attached | The location is `@mentioned` only in the seeded-only `scenePrompt` (or pulled via `locationId`), so lint counts it — but on the default path only `motionPrompt` text is sent | `@mention` the location in the `motionPrompt` TEXT itself (every shot of a multi-shot clip). A clean lint off the `scenePrompt` is NOT a `motionPrompt` anchor |
| A rewrite quietly loses an `@mention` that was in the old prompt | Rewriting/trimming `motionPrompt` dropped a tag; lint stayed clean off `scenePrompt` | After any rewrite, diff vs the old `motionPrompt` and confirm every entity is still `@mentioned` in the new text |
| The clip renders the WRONG angle of the right location — the beat's props (the side table, the lamp) aren't in frame, though `shotkit lint` was clean | A bare `@loc` and a `@loc#view` of the same location were both mentioned in one clip. Mentions resolve to ONE view per entity and an explicit `#label` beats a bare mention anywhere in the text — so only the labelled view attached. The scene's `locationId` does not rescue it either: it only anchors a location that isn't already mentioned | Don't mix bare + `#view` for one location in a clip. Pick the single view the beat needs; for two specific angles promote one to its **own location entity** (`@foyer` + `@foyer_stairs`) so the de-dup can't collapse them; `@loc*` for every view (budget permitting). Check `out/scenes/<scene-id>/motion.refs.txt` (or `frame.refs.txt`), not the mention count |
| A view attached via `@loc*` gets dropped or blended once the clip is over the target engine's ref cap | shotkit puts every stored view on the list in mention order and does not trim anything itself — a full `@loc*` set can push the clip past the cap, and it's the target engine's own (unpredictable) behavior that decides what gets dropped or blended past that point | Give the angle its own location entity instead of relying on `*` — that guarantees exactly the two frames you want are in the list, at fixed positions. Or cut a character/prop mention, or split the beat into two scenes |
| Location renders as a split-screen / multiple frames in one shot | A multi-panel board/collage was anchored as the location reference | Anchor a SINGLE clean establishing frame per view (generate it with `shotkit location <id> --view <label>`, save one clean frame) — never a grid/contact-sheet |
| A hall/classroom renders EMPTY even though the prompt/canonical says "full of students" | Wording alone won't populate a reference-conditioned space; the location ref itself may show an empty room | Force the crowd in EVERY shot of the `motionPrompt`, or anchor a real POPULATED photo as the location reference. A "only N seats left" beat needs a seeded keyframe to carve the gaps — a fully-packed plate breaks it |
| The background crowd renders FROZEN (mannequin extras, no one moving) | A populated ref/prompt gives bodies but no motion cue for them | Add a LIFE clause: "the crowd is alive from the first frame, no one motionless — small shifts, murmurs, turning heads; only X stands, everyone else stays seated" |
| Clip renders SPLIT into two stacked panels (top half one shot, bottom half another) | Prompt said "stacked two-shot" / "split" / "panel" / "side-by-side", OR a multi-shot prompt got read as showing the shots *simultaneously* | Remove the literal words. State "ONE continuous full frame at all times, never a split screen, never stacked panels". Describe vertical staging as position-in-one-frame. For cuts, say "hard cuts in TIME only — the whole frame changes on each cut" |
| A character renders in their PRIMARY outfit even though the scene tags a look (`@hero#wet`) | The label matched no look **with a `refImage` set**, so shotkit fell back to the primary look. This fallback is deliberate — it is what keeps pre-looks scenes rendering — so it will not stop a render | Read the warning: shotkit prints `@hero#wet: no look labelled "wet" … — using the primary look`. Then check the label matches **exactly** — labels are matched whole, so a look labelled `wet jacket` is NOT selected by `#wet`; prefer one-word labels. If the look exists in `bible.json` but was never rendered, run `shotkit sheet <id> --look wet`, generate it in your image model, save the result, and point that look's `refImage` at it before mentioning it |
| An `@mention` attaches the WRONG entity's reference — a location/prop where you meant the character | A possessive. Mentions match full roster **names**, longest-first and case-insensitively, so a roster entry whose name contains an apostrophe swallows the `'s`: with a character `Cleo` and a location `Cleo's Auto`, **`@Cleo's auto door` resolves to the LOCATION**. Spelling the mention by id (`@cleo's`) does NOT dodge it — the name still matches | Look at the chip: the editor renders whatever the generator will actually attach, so a wrong resolve shows as a wrong name/colour before you spend a render. Fix by rephrasing so no possessive follows the tag — "the auto door of `@cleo`", not "`@Cleo's` auto door". The durable fix is to keep apostrophes out of roster names |

## Moderation refusals — the job never rendered

The provider classifies the **assembled** prompt (your field + the injected who-is-who + the
audio tail) before generating, and refuses with one opaque line that names nothing:
`Content flagged as potentially sensitive.` It scores words that are present and does not
parse negation. Doctrine and the bisection procedure: SKILL.md → "The moderation gate".

| Symptom | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| `Content flagged as potentially sensitive` on a prompt with nothing sexual or violent in it | A **negated** constraint: `NO fire, NO smoke`, `no restyling`, `fully covered and modest`, `no clothing`. The classifier reads `fire` / `clothing` / `modest` and ignores the `no` | Rewrite every lock as a positive invariant — `clear untouched air`, `costume unchanged across shots`, `she wears full flight-leathers, high collar, gloves`. Same rule as the LOCKS block |
| The same scene is refused no matter how the shot lines are rewritten | The trigger is in the **injected** text, not the field being edited — a `canonicalDescription` ending `Identity only — no clothing here.` is prepended to every clip with 2+ characters | Fix the character's `canonicalDescription` at the source: description only, no directives addressed to its author. One fix clears every scene that character is in |
| Every scene with one particular character is refused; scenes without them render fine | That character's `canonicalDescription` combines an **age in years, a body description and a romance beat** (`a delicate young woman of nineteen`, `slight, light build`) — the minor-safety axis, lowest threshold, no appeal | Drop the number and the diminutives (`delicate`, `youthful`, `slight`, `the youngest`); keep hair, eyes, bone structure, skin. The sheet carries the face, not the adjective |
| A flight/riding/horseback scene is refused although nothing sexual was written | Blocking language that doubles as a sex position — `mounted directly behind her`, `on top of him`, `straddling` — is read on its own terms, saddle or not | Name the seat, not the arrangement: `@wren in the forward saddle, @cassian in the rear saddle` |
| Refusals on a project whose roster names carry roles or franchises | Names are injected verbatim (every `@mention` resolves to them): `Wren (heroine)` — `heroine` contains `heroin`, a keyword-filter hit — and `Royal Dragon (House of Dragons)` puts a near-miss of a real title in every prompt | Rename the roster entries to plain one/two-word names; keep roles and house lore in your notes. The parentheses render nothing anyway |
| The prompt was cleaned and it still refuses | The **images** are moderated separately by many providers — on a seeded mode the keyframe and every reference are scored too, and no text edit clears an image flag | Cut back to a bare `@mention` set, confirm it renders, then add references one at a time until it refuses again |
| It failed, then the identical prompt succeeded on a retry | These classifiers are not deterministic; a borderline score sometimes passes | Do not ship on the retry — the prompt is still over the line and the next render is another coin-flip. Fix the wording, then re-submit |

## Loops, framing, audio, ops

| Symptom | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Idle-loop clip jumps visibly each time it repeats | Authored as a normal one-way clip, not a seamless loop | Set `loop:true`, author ONE held take that returns to the start pose, prefer a seeded mode so frame 0 is a fixed anchor, only reversible idle motion. See "Looping / idle scenes" |
| Camera feels stuck / no reveal | Animated from a locked keyframe (`i2v` or `ref-anchored --keyframe`) | Use the default no-keyframe path (`--mode t2v`) — frees the camera |
| Key beat hidden behind UI/subtitle | Staged in the bottom third in 9:16 | Move the subject/info into the upper two-thirds |
| Lip-sync wrong / too fast | `dialogue` words exceed ~2/sec of duration | Trim spoken words or raise `durationSec` |
| The off-screen narrator's line renders as the on-camera character SAYING it, lip-synced — the baked narrator became direct speech | A `VO:` segment and a plain `dialogue` line landed on the SAME shot: both anchored to it, or both left unanchored (the engine then places them together). Both are appended to the motionPrompt as speech clauses for that window, and with a face in frame the engine syncs the narration to it | Give the narration its own shot **inside the same scene** — an insert, a reaction, a listening beat, a wide with nobody speaking — add that shot to the motionPrompt's shot list, and anchor it there (`VO: [shot 4] …`) with the spoken line anchored to a different shot. Never leave both kinds unanchored. A single held take has only `SHOT 1`: split it in two, or move the narration out of the scene |
